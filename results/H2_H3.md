## H2: the stability ratio

**Hypothesis (spec section 2).** Where tickets fail, S(t) = eta_t · lambda_max(t) / (2 + 2·beta) reaches about 1 in early training. Where they win,
S stays clearly below 1. Sharpness at initialization alone is not expected to predict this.

**Statistic.** max S over steps 25–3,000. S(0) is reported separately (deviation in `Methodology.md` section 9).
Points are at steps 0, 25, 50, 100, 200, then every 500 to 3,000 (D11).

### Results

**1. No run comes near S = 1 during training.** Over 56 tickets and 32 baselines (s0+s1, as of 06:45 UTC), the largest max S(25–3k) is 0.54
(high, seed 0, 70.1%). The failing high tickets peak at 0.16–0.54, a factor of 2–6 below the threshold. As stated, H2 fails.

**2. S does separate the failing condition from the winning ones, at the condition level** (tickets, s0+s1):

| Condition | Winning tickets | max S(25–3k) | S at step 25 | S at step 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high | none (0/7 baseline points) | 0.16–0.54 | 0.13–0.54 | 0.09–0.17 |
| low | 6/7 so far (r8 s0 is 0.53 pp below dense) | 0.06–0.15 | 0.04–0.08 | 0.05–0.09 |
| warm03 | 4/4 | 0.03–0.08 | ≤ 0.001 | ≤ 0.01 |
| high_warm | 3/4 | 0.05–0.09 | ≤ 0.003 | 0.02–0.05 |

The ranges do not overlap: the lowest high value (0.16) is above the highest in any winning condition (0.15).
Across all ticket runs, the Spearman correlation between max S and the ticket's gap to dense is ρ = −0.65 (n = 49, p < 1e-6).
That correlation is entirely between conditions, though (point 4).

**3. The difference is a short transient right after the start.** In high, max S(25–3k) falls at **step 25 in 15 of 18 tickets**,
and by step 200 S is 0.09–0.17. After step 1,000, S is 0.02–0.09 in every condition, with or without warmup.
In the warmup runs, max S falls at steps 500–2,500, as the LR ramps. In low it falls at steps 25–500.
Dense high seed 0: lambda_max goes from 25.5 to 6.2 within 25 steps and to ~2 by step 1,000. Seed 1 starts sharper
(lambda_max(theta_0) = 40.6 vs 25.5) and reaches 18.8 at step 25 and 4.5 at step 200.

**4. Within a condition, S does not predict ticket quality.** Spearman ρ(max S, gap to dense) by condition, over rounds 1–8:
- high +0.26 (n = 16);
- low −0.38 (n = 16);
- warm03 −0.04 (n = 9);
- high_warm −0.31 (n = 8).

In high, the deficit grows from −0.3 to −7.1 pp with sparsity while max S stays flat at 0.16–0.31 from 49% down.
**S(0) is even *anti*-predictive within high** (ρ = +0.86): sparser masks start flatter (S(0) 0.67 → 0.13 on seed 0) and fail worse.
Since S(0) and sparsity move together, this mostly shows that sharpness at initialization tracks the mask density, not the failure.
That agrees with H2's own caveat and with pilot P2.

**5. The trained baselines at eta = 0.1 have the same or higher S than the tickets** (best-baseline max S vs ticket, 7 masks, s0+s1):
- the baseline is higher at 6 of 7 masks;
- by mask: 0.33 vs 0.24, 0.31 vs 0.31, 0.27 vs 0.18, 0.26 vs 0.29, 0.35 vs 0.28, 0.39 vs 0.21 and 0.29 vs 0.25.

Neither the ticket nor the baselines win at eta = 0.1 (advantage ≤ +0.67 pp), so S at the mask level cannot explain a ticket-specific failure.
That would need a mask where S differs and the advantage differs. H4 provides such masks.

**6. The threshold itself is uncertain, which limits the "S ≈ 1" reading.** From Kalra & Barkeshli (2024), §4.2 and App. E.2:
- **At initialization**, SGD with momentum becomes unstable at eta·lambda = 2, not 2 + 2·beta, because the momentum buffer is empty.
  Against 2/lambda, dense high starts at 1.27 (seed 0) and 2.03 (seed 1), above the threshold.
  Every high ticket with S(0) > 0.526 also starts above it (seed 0 rounds 0–3, seed 1 rounds 0–3).
  The tickets that fail worst (≤ 12% remaining) start *below* it (0.25–0.93 against 2/lambda), so initial instability does not track the deficit.
- **Later in training**, the minibatch threshold is "much smaller" than (2 + 2·beta)/lambda. So the common plateau at S ≈ 0.03–0.05 after step 1,000
  may be the batch-128 edge of stability, and "S stays far below 1" may partly reflect the full-batch threshold we divide by.
  We did not measure the minibatch threshold.
- **The loss curve shows one catapult**, in the self-stabilization sense: dense high seed 0, at rounds 0–1, where the minibatch loss
  rises to 1.44x and 1.51x its running minimum at step 4. No other run rebounds above 1.3x in its first 10 steps, except one baseline (high seed 1, r2 reinit: 1.30x at step 10).
  Everywhere else, the largest rebound in the first 200 steps (1.1–1.5x) comes later, at steps 90–200. low tickets show the same
  rebounds (1.23–1.54x), so this is minibatch noise, not instability. Seed 1, which starts further above 2/lambda, shows no step-4 rebound.

### H2 verdict (draft): **not supported as stated**; a weaker, condition-level association holds

- **What fails:** "S reaches ~1 where tickets fail." No run reaches 0.6, and within a condition S does not order the tickets.
- **What holds:** without warmup at eta = 0.1, the first ~200 steps run at 2–6x the S of every winning condition, and only that condition fails.
  The time window matches the early-instability story (Kalra & Barkeshli; Frankle et al. 2020 on early-training instability).
  The data, though, cannot tell a causal role for that transient apart from anything else that differs in high's first steps.
- **Open:** H4(b) (SAM) is the planned test. SAM cannot act before the first gradient steps (`results/H4.md` §4), so it tests
  the later, sustained part of the sharpness story better than the step-25 transient.

## H3: the warmup mechanism

**Hypothesis (spec section 2).** Warmup keeps S below 1 through the high-learning-rate phase, and this coincides with the return of the
ticket advantage.

### Results

**1. The coincidence holds.** At the same target LR, eta = 0.1 (seed 0):
- with warmup (high_warm), the advantage is +1.47, +3.45, +5.11 and +5.93 pp at 49.1, 24.2, 12.0 and 6.0%: 3 winning tickets out of 4;
- without warmup (high), it is +0.36, +0.13, +0.39 and −0.03 pp: none.

high_warm's max S(25–3k) is 0.05–0.09, against high's 0.16–0.54.

**2. "Keeps S below 1" is not what separates them, because high stays below 1 too** (H2 point 1). What warmup removes is the
step-25 transient: S at step 25 is ≤ 0.003 under high_warm, against 0.13–0.54 under high.

**3. During the high-LR phase itself, warmup and no-warmup look alike.** At step 9,000, where high_warm's LR is 0.09, lambda_max is 1.3–1.7
and S 0.03–0.04 (rounds 0, 3, 6 and 8). The high chain at the same step has lambda_max 1.1–1.5 and S 0.03–0.04. Both have flattened to the same
plateau by the time the LR is large. The difference is in *how* they get there: high abruptly within ~25 steps, high_warm gradually,
starting at step ≈ 500 when its LR passes ≈ 0.005 (dense lambda_max 36.6 at step 500 → 11.3 at 2k → 6.1 at 3k → 2.4 at 6k).

**4. Warmup first lets sharpness grow** (progressive sharpening; Kalra & Barkeshli 2024 expect it "especially for prolonged warmups").
- warm03: dense lambda_max 25.5 → 52.7 at step 2,000. The small LR holds S ≤ 0.08, and lambda_max then falls to ~4 as the LR grows.
- high_warm: the sharpening stops earlier (peak 36.6 at step 500).
- In both, the turn-over happens at S ≈ 0.08–0.09, far below 1. That also argues against a fixed S = 1 edge as the operative threshold.
  It is consistent with the minibatch threshold sitting much lower (H2 point 6).

**5. Confounds that H3 alone cannot remove:**
- Warmup also keeps more weight correlation: dense R_0.2 is 0.331 for high_warm vs 0.247 for high (warm03 0.558, low 0.640).
- Warmup halves the mean LR over the first 10k steps (0.05 vs 0.1). In our schedule, the warmup ends at the first decay
  (as in Frankle & Carbin; see `PHASE_A.md` §3), so high_warm never trains at a sustained eta = 0.1.
- So the return of the advantage coincides with lower early S **and** higher R_p **and** a lower average LR. H4 separates the first two.

### H3 verdict (draft): **partly supported**

- Warmup does coincide with the return of the ticket advantage at eta = 0.1, and with the removal of the early S transient.
- The mechanism as stated ("keeps S below 1") does not discriminate, since the failing condition is also below 1.
- Whether the transient, rather than the correlation or the lower mean LR, is what matters is left to H4.
