# H4 write-up (fixed masks, eta = 0.1)

*Status: §1–5 drafted on 2026-10-01, 05:55 UTC, and **not changed since**; §6–7 (results, verdict) added on 2026-10-02 after all 18 runs. Original note: draft written **before any H4 result exists**. The design, the predictions and the proposed
decision rule are fixed here first, so the results cannot shape them. The H4 runs start ≈ 07:25 UTC and end ≈ 12:30 UTC. Cells marked
"—" are filled in when they land. All numbers will come from `results/summary.csv` (`python -m analysis.plots`, fig. 5).*

## 1. Question

Phase A gave two readings that the observational data cannot separate (`results/PHASE_A.md` §4–6):
- **Sharpness.** The failing `high` tickets have the highest early S (max S(25–3k) 0.17–0.54, against ≤ 0.15 everywhere else),
  but nowhere near S = 1.
- **Weight correlation (Liu et al. 2021).** `high` decorrelates theta_T from theta_0 (R_0.2 ≈ 0.22–0.25, near chance), but
  the winning high_warm and warm03 tickets reach equally low R_0.2 at high sparsity.

H4 intervenes on each variable separately, with the mask held fixed.

## 2. Design

- **Masks:** warm03 seed 0 at rounds 3, 6 and 8 (34.5%, 12.0% and 6.0% remaining). These masks win under warm03.
  **theta_0** is warm03 seed 0's, the same theta_0 every seed-0 chain uses.
- **Training:** eta = 0.1, no warmup, otherwise identical to `high` (15k iterations, decay at 10k / 12.5k, batch 128, momentum 0.9).
- **Variants:** the ticket (theta_0 ⊙ m) and a shuffle baseline (theta_0 permuted within each layer over the mask).
  The shuffle uses the same permutation seed as warm03's own shuffle at that round (`seed + 100·round`).
- **Three conditions** (spec section 2, H4), 3 masks × 2 variants each, 18 trainings in all:

| Condition | Config | Change | Intended effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) plain | `h4_high` | none | the reference at eta = 0.1 |
| (b) SAM | `h4_high_sam` | SAM, rho = 0.05 (Foret et al. 2021, Alg. 1); perturbation restricted to the mask; ~2x cost | lowers sharpness; R_p is not targeted |
| (c) anchor | `h4_high_anchor` | + (lam/2)·‖m ⊙ (theta − theta_0)‖² | raises R_p; sharpness is not targeted |

- **Anchor lam selection (D6), done before the anchor runs.** A 3k-step run at the round-3 mask for each lam in {1e-3, 1e-2}.
  The target is the `low` seed-0 round-3 ticket's R_0.2 at step 3,000, which is **0.661**. The code (`src/fixed_mask.py::select_lam`) takes the
  **smallest lam that reaches the target**. If neither does, it takes the lam with the highest R_0.2 and records `reached_target: false`.
  The result goes to `results/h4_high_anchor/lam_selection.json`.
- **The anchor does not lower sharpness, by construction.** It adds lam ≤ 0.01 to the Hessian on the mask, which is negligible against
  lambda_max ≈ 2–50 (Phase A). So any change in lambda_max under the anchor comes from the training path, not from the penalty term.
- **One seed** (0). There is no budget for a second H4 seed (≈ 4 h with SAM).

## 4. What each outcome would mean

**First, (a) on its own separates the mask from the training dynamics**, an alternative that Phase A cannot rule out:
- **If warm03's masks still win at eta = 0.1 with no warmup** (ticket − shuffle advantage comparable to warm03's), then high's failure comes from the
  *masks IMP finds at eta = 0.1*, not from how a good mask trains at eta = 0.1. H2/H3, which are about training dynamics, would then not explain the
  ticket failure. This connects to Paul et al. (2023) on what IMP masks encode. (b) and (c) then become secondary.
- **If they fail at eta = 0.1** (advantage near the high chain's ≤ +0.7 pp), then training at eta = 0.1 is enough to break even a good mask,
  and (b) vs (c) addresses the mechanism.

**Then (b) vs (c)**, applying the spec's rule to whatever counts as "rescue" (section 5):

| (b) SAM rescues? | (c) anchor rescues? | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| yes | no | supports sharpness (H2/H3) |
| no | yes | supports weight correlation (Liu et al.) |
| yes | yes | both contribute |
| no | no | neither intervention, at these strengths, explains the failure |

**Known limits of the test, stated in advance:**
- **SAM cannot change the first steps.** Phase A puts the sharpness difference in the first ~200 steps, and for dense high in the first 25
  (lambda_max 25.5 → 6.2; `PHASE_A.md` §4). SAM with rho = 0.05 changes the gradient, not the step size, so it cannot remove an instability at
  step 0. A "no" for SAM is therefore weaker evidence against sharpness than a "yes" is for it.
- **The anchor may not reach its R_p target.** The target of 0.661 is a low-LR correlation level. At eta = 0.1, lam ≤ 1e-2 may fall short, in which
  case (c) raises R_p only partly. Report the achieved R_0.2 next to the verdict.
- **SAM also raises accuracy in general** (Foret et al. 2021). So "ticket ≥ some dense − 0.5 pp" is not a fair rescue test for (b) without
  a dense SAM run, which is not in the plan. Hence the advantage-based rule below.
- One seed. Phase A seed-to-seed differences in ticket accuracy are ≈ 0.1–0.4 pp (low/high, seed 0 vs 1), and in advantage ≈ 0.05–0.4 pp (low and high, rounds 2, 4 and 6).

## 5. Decision rule (approved by the user on 2026-10-01, before any H4 result; D13)

The spec says "rescues" without defining it. This rule was fixed before the data, and it is not changed after seeing them.

- **Primary statistic:** ticket − shuffle advantage at each of the 3 masks.
- **Rescue:** the advantage is **≥ +2.0 pp at ≥ 2 of the 3 masks.** For calibration:
  - the high chain never exceeds +0.7 pp at any mask (seed 0 max +0.57 vs shuffle at 6.0%; seed 1 +0.67 vs reinit at 12.0%);
  - warm03's smallest advantage over its best baseline is +1.91 pp (49.1%), and +7.60 pp vs shuffle at 6.0%.

  So 2 pp sits well above the failing condition and at the low edge of the winning ones, and about 5x the single-seed noise.
- **Also reported, not used in the verdict:** max S(25–3k), S(0), R_0.2 (final and at step 3,000), the anchor's achieved R_0.2 vs target,
  and the ticket's accuracy vs dense `high` (89.83%).
- **Was the intervention effective?** A verdict for (b) needs SAM's max S(25–3k) to fall below (a)'s at the same mask. A verdict for (c)
  needs the anchor's R_0.2 at step 3,000 to rise above (a)'s. If the manipulation did not move its target variable, the result is
  "inconclusive", not "does not rescue".

## 7. Verdict

**(a) warm03's masks still win at eta = 0.1 without warmup: +3.59 and +4.80 pp at 12.0% and 6.0% (2 of 3 masks ≥ +2.0 pp; D13).**
By the rule in §4, decided before the data, this is the first branch: **high's failure comes from the masks IMP finds at eta = 0.1, not from how a
good mask trains at eta = 0.1.** (b) and (c) are therefore secondary, and their "rescue" labels mean little, because in (a) there is no failure to rescue.

What supports this reading:
- **The same training run, different mask, opposite result.** At eta = 0.1 with no warmup, warm03's 6.0% mask gives 87.24%; high's own 6.0% mask gives
  82.72% (−4.5 pp), while the shuffled baselines are equal (82.4% vs 82.2%).
- **The early sharpness transient is present and the ticket still wins.** (a)'s max S(25–3k) is 0.24–0.34, inside the high chain's range
  (0.17–0.54) and 6–9x warm03's at the same masks (0.036–0.043). So the transient that separates high from the winning conditions in Phase A (H2 point 2) does not prevent winning.
- **Weight correlation is lost and the ticket still wins.** (a)'s R_0.2 is 0.18–0.20 at the end and 0.22–0.30 at step 3,000, near chance and no higher
  than in the failing high chain (0.22–0.24). So "the ticket fails because training at eta = 0.1 decorrelates it from theta_0" (Liu et al. 2021) does not hold either.

**(b) SAM** lowered max S(25–3k) below (a) at all 3 masks (0.19–0.22 vs 0.24–0.34), the manipulation check passed, and the advantage did not move:
+1.15 / +3.22 / +5.26 vs (a)'s +0.89 / +3.59 / +4.80 (differences ≤ 0.5 pp, within single-seed noise). Ticket accuracy is within 0.3 pp of (a). **Lowering
sharpness after the first steps changes neither the ticket nor its advantage.** (As stated in §4, SAM cannot act on S(0), which is the same in every condition.)

**(c) The anchor** raised R_0.2 at 3k above (a) at all 3 masks (0.54–0.75 vs 0.22–0.30), so the manipulation check passed. Its advantage is the largest
(+7.6 to +12.3 pp), but not because the ticket improved: the anchored tickets lose 2.7–3.6 pp against (a), and the anchored shuffles lose 10.0–10.4 pp.
Pulling the weights toward a random (shuffled) theta_0 hurts far more than pulling them toward the ticket's own theta_0. That says the ticket's
theta_0 suits its mask, which is what the lottery ticket hypothesis claims, but **forcing correlation does not make the ticket better.**

**Limits.**
- One seed. The verdicts for (a) and (b) rest on advantages of +3.2 to +5.3 pp. With all seed-1 runs in, the seed-to-seed spread of advantages
  is ≤ 0.6 pp, except warm03 at 6.0% (2.0 pp; at 12.0% it is ≤ 0.3 pp). So the 12.0% result is unlikely to flip; the 6.0% one is less certain. At 34.5% the advantage is below +2.0 pp in (a) and (b); warm03's own advantage was never measured there (no baseline at round 3).
- **(a) is a win under the D13 advantage rule, not under the section-5 winning-ticket definition.** The tickets are 0.56, 0.92 and 2.59 pp below
  dense high (89.83%), so at eta = 0.1 even warm03's masks don't fully match the dense network. Some of the eta = 0.1 cost survives a good mask:
  the advantage is also smaller than in warm03 itself (+3.59 vs +4.89 vs reinit at 12.0%; +4.80 vs +7.60 vs shuffle at 6.0%).
- Only warm03's masks were tested. That high's own masks fail under SAM or warmup was not tested here (high_warm, Phase A, finds its own masks).

**For H2/H3** (`results/H2_H3.md`): the causal test goes against the training-dynamics readings. A good mask wins through the same early S transient
and the same loss of correlation that accompany the failing high chain. What differs is the mask, which brings the question to
how IMP's pruning at eta = 0.1 picks masks (Paul et al. 2023, on what IMP masks encode). Phase A cannot tell whether the warmup's role (H3)
is to produce good masks during IMP rather than to train a given mask better. high_warm's winning masks fit that reading, but it was not tested directly.

## 8. Follow-up runs (pre-registered on 2026-10-02, 16:20 UTC, before either has started)

Section 7 rests on one seed, and "the mask is the cause" was inferred, not tested. The user approved two follow-ups (12 trainings, ≈ 2 h,
within the remaining budget). Same code (`src/fixed_mask.py`), same rule (D13: ticket − shuffle ≥ +2.0 pp at ≥ 2 of 3 masks).

**F1. Replication of (a) on seed 1.** `configs/h4_high.yaml --seeds 1`: warm03 **seed 1**'s masks (rounds 3, 6, 8) and seed 1's theta_0, at
eta = 0.1 with no warmup.
- Prediction if §7 holds: ≥ +2.0 pp at ≥ 2 of 3 masks again.
- If it fails, the (a) verdict does not replicate, and §7 is reported as one-seed only.

**F2. Reverse swap.** `configs/h4_swap_high_warm.yaml`: the **high** chain's own seed-0 masks (rounds 3, 6, 8), with the same theta_0 as (a)
(checked: high and warm03 seed 0 share theta_0 bit for bit). They are trained on the high_warm schedule (eta = 0.1, 10k-step warmup).
- **Mask reading (§7) predicts failure:** < +2.0 pp at ≥ 2 of 3 masks, as in the high chain (≤ +0.7 pp).
- **If ≥ +2.0 pp at ≥ 2 of 3 masks, warmup rescues high's masks.** Then the mask is not the whole story: warmup also helps a given (bad) mask,
  and §7's "warmup acts by finding the mask" is wrong.
- Also reported: ticket accuracy vs the same masks without warmup (high chain: 88.36 / 85.52 / 82.72%) and vs high_warm's own tickets.
