## 1. Question

Phase A gave two readings that the observational data cannot separate (`results/PHASE_A.md` §4–6):
- **Sharpness.** The failing `high` tickets have the highest early S (max S(25–3k) 0.17–0.54, against ≤ 0.15 everywhere else),
  but nowhere near S = 1.
- **Weight correlation (Liu et al. 2021).** `high` decorrelates theta_T from theta_0 (R_0.2 ≈ 0.22–0.25, near chance), but
  the winning high_warm and warm03 tickets reach equally low R_0.2 at high sparsity.

H4 intervenes on each variable separately, with the mask held fixed.

## 2. Design

- **Masks:** warm03 seed 0 at rounds 3, 6 and 8 (34.5%, 12.0% and 6.0% remaining). These masks win under warm03.
  **theta_0** is warm03 seed 0's, the same theta_0 every seed-0 chain uses.
- **Training:** eta = 0.1, no warmup, otherwise identical to `high` (15k iterations, decay at 10k / 12.5k, batch 128, momentum 0.9).
- **Variants:** the ticket (theta_0 ⊙ m) and a shuffle baseline (theta_0 permuted within each layer over the mask).
  The shuffle uses the same permutation seed as warm03's own shuffle at that round (`seed + 100·round`).
- **Three conditions** (spec section 2, H4), 3 masks × 2 variants each, 18 trainings in all:

| Condition | Config | Change | Intended effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) plain | `h4_high` | none | the reference at eta = 0.1 |
| (b) SAM | `h4_high_sam` | SAM, rho = 0.05 (Foret et al. 2021, Alg. 1); perturbation restricted to the mask; ~2x cost | lowers sharpness; R_p is not targeted |
| (c) anchor | `h4_high_anchor` | + (lam/2)·‖m ⊙ (theta − theta_0)‖² | raises R_p; sharpness is not targeted |

- **Anchor lam selection (D6), done before the anchor runs.** A 3k-step run at the round-3 mask for each lam in {1e-3, 1e-2}.
  The target is the `low` seed-0 round-3 ticket's R_0.2 at step 3,000, which is **0.661**. The code (`src/fixed_mask.py::select_lam`) takes the
  **smallest lam that reaches the target**. If neither does, it takes the lam with the highest R_0.2 and records `reached_target: false`.
  The result goes to `results/h4_high_anchor/lam_selection.json`.
- **The anchor does not lower sharpness, by construction.** It adds lam ≤ 0.01 to the Hessian on the mask, which is negligible against
  lambda_max ≈ 2–50 (Phase A). So any change in lambda_max under the anchor comes from the training path, not from the penalty term.
- **One seed** (0). There is no budget for a second H4 seed (≈ 4 h with SAM).

## 4. What each outcome would mean

**First, (a) on its own separates the mask from the training dynamics**, an alternative that Phase A cannot rule out:
- **If warm03's masks still win at eta = 0.1 with no warmup** (ticket − shuffle advantage comparable to warm03's), then high's failure comes from the
  *masks IMP finds at eta = 0.1*, not from how a good mask trains at eta = 0.1. H2/H3, which are about training dynamics, would then not explain the
  ticket failure. This connects to Paul et al. (2023) on what IMP masks encode. (b) and (c) then become secondary.
- **If they fail at eta = 0.1** (advantage near the high chain's ≤ +0.7 pp), then training at eta = 0.1 is enough to break even a good mask,
  and (b) vs (c) addresses the mechanism.

**Then (b) vs (c)**, applying the spec's rule to whatever counts as "rescue" (section 5):

| (b) SAM rescues? | (c) anchor rescues? | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| yes | no | supports sharpness (H2/H3) |
| no | yes | supports weight correlation (Liu et al.) |
| yes | yes | both contribute |
| no | no | neither intervention, at these strengths, explains the failure |

**Known limits of the test, stated in advance:**
- **SAM cannot change the first steps.** Phase A puts the sharpness difference in the first ~200 steps, and for dense high in the first 25
  (lambda_max 25.5 → 6.2; `PHASE_A.md` §4). SAM with rho = 0.05 changes the gradient, not the step size, so it cannot remove an instability at
  step 0. A "no" for SAM is therefore weaker evidence against sharpness than a "yes" is for it.
- **The anchor may not reach its R_p target.** The target of 0.661 is a low-LR correlation level. At eta = 0.1, lam ≤ 1e-2 may fall short, in which
  case (c) raises R_p only partly. Report the achieved R_0.2 next to the verdict.
- **SAM also raises accuracy in general** (Foret et al. 2021). So "ticket ≥ some dense − 0.5 pp" is not a fair rescue test for (b) without
  a dense SAM run, which is not in the plan. Hence the advantage-based rule below.
- One seed. Phase A seed-to-seed differences in ticket accuracy are ≈ 0.1–0.4 pp (low/high, seed 0 vs 1), and in advantage ≈ 0.05–0.4 pp (low and high, rounds 2, 4 and 6).

## 5. Proposed decision rule (needs user approval before the H4 results land)

The spec says "rescues" without defining it, so this rule is a proposal. Changing it after seeing the data would not be allowed.

- **Primary statistic:** ticket − shuffle advantage at each of the 3 masks.
- **Rescue:** the advantage is **≥ +2.0 pp at ≥ 2 of the 3 masks.** For calibration:
  - the high chain never exceeds +0.7 pp at any mask (seed 0 max +0.57 vs shuffle at 6.0%; seed 1 +0.67 vs reinit at 12.0%);
  - warm03's smallest advantage over its best baseline is +1.91 pp (49.1%), and +7.60 pp vs shuffle at 6.0%.

  So 2 pp sits well above the failing condition and at the low edge of the winning ones, and about 5x the single-seed noise.
- **Also reported, not used in the verdict:** max S(25–3k), S(0), R_0.2 (final and at step 3,000), the anchor's achieved R_0.2 vs target,
  and the ticket's accuracy vs dense `high` (89.83%).
- **Was the intervention effective?** A verdict for (b) needs SAM's max S(25–3k) to fall below (a)'s at the same mask. A verdict for (c)
  needs the anchor's R_0.2 at step 3,000 to rise above (a)'s. If the manipulation did not move its target variable, the result is
  "inconclusive", not "does not rescue".
